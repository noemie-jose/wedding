Travel & stay

Getting to Annecy

Fly into Geneva (GVA). Annecy sits just south of it, across the French border, about 45 minutes by car, or a direct coach from the airport. It's the easiest arrival for almost everyone.

From Geneva Airport to Annecy

OptionTimeNotes
Car or taxi≈ 45 minAbout 45 km. Rental desks are in the airport's French sector, which saves you the border crossing. Taxis and pre-booked transfers run around €110-150.
Direct coach≈ 1 h 15Cars Région Haute-Savoie line to Annecy, plus FlixBus and BlaBlaCar Bus. Roughly hourly, around €10-15, straight from the airport to Annecy's bus station.
Train≈ 2 h+Doable, but it means a change at Annemasse or Bellegarde. Take the coach instead unless you love a train.
Shared ride≈ 45 minBlaBlaCar is widely used on this route and cheap if the timing works.

Geneva airport straddles the border, so follow signs for the French sector if you're renting a car or taking a French coach.

Flights

Typical routings from where most of you are starting. Airlines shuffle their schedules every season, so treat this as a starting point and check current timetables when you book.

FromNonstop to Geneva?How people usually do it
Boston (BOS) No One stop, about 10-12 hours door to door. SWISS via Zurich, Lufthansa via Frankfurt or Munich, Air France via Paris, KLM or Delta via Amsterdam, Aer Lingus via Dublin, TAP via Lisbon. An overnight out, a daytime flight home.
New York (JFK / EWR) Yes SWISS flies JFK-Geneva nonstop, about 7h50. United flies Newark-Geneva nonstop in the summer season (roughly May-October), so September is usually still running. Otherwise one stop via Zurich, Paris, Amsterdam or Frankfurt.
Portland, Oregon (PDX) No Two legs. KLM/Delta via Amsterdam is the shortest when the seasonal PDX-AMS flight is operating; otherwise connect in Seattle, San Francisco, Newark or Chicago and pick up a nonstop from there. Lufthansa and Air France route via Frankfurt and Paris.
Brussels (BRU) Yes Brussels Airlines and easyJet, about 1h10, several a day. By train it's roughly 6-7 hours via Paris (Eurostar to Gare du Nord, then a TGV from Gare de Lyon). A good option if you'd rather not fly.
Amsterdam (AMS) Yes KLM and easyJet, about 1h30, many flights a day.
Barcelona (BCN) Yes Vueling, easyJet and SWISS, about 1h30.

If Geneva Doesn't Work

  • Lyon (LYS): about 1h45 by car, and sometimes much cheaper from within Europe.
  • Paris (CDG or ORY): a direct TGV from Paris Gare de Lyon to Annecy, roughly 3h45.
  • Zurich (ZRH): about 4 hours by train or 3h30 by car, worth checking if the fare is right.

Staying in Town

Annecy's old town puts you a walk from dinner, the lake and the Friday drinks. It's the simplest choice if you're not renting a car.

On the Lake

Villages like Veyrier-du-Lac, Menthon-Saint-Bernard and Talloires are 10-20 minutes out, quieter, and right on the water. Good if you're a group sharing a house.

Book Early

September is still high season by the lake and rooms go fast. Hold something refundable now, and move into the block when it opens if that's a better rate.

Practicalities

Documents

France is in the Schengen area. US, UK and most non-EU visitors don't need a visa for a short stay, but the EU's new ETIAS travel authorisation is expected to be in force by 2027: a quick online form and a small fee. Check the official rules a few months before you fly, and make sure your passport is valid for at least six months past the trip.

Money & Phones

France uses the euro; Geneva uses Swiss francs, so don't stock up on the wrong one in transit. Cards work nearly everywhere. EU roaming is free for EU numbers; US carriers charge, so check your plan.

Weather in September

Usually lovely: mid-20s °C (mid-70s °F) in the afternoon, dropping to around 10 °C (50 °F) at night. Bring a layer for the evening and something you can walk cobblestones in.

Language

Annecy is French-speaking and the tourist-facing world speaks English. A bonjour on the way in goes a long way.
